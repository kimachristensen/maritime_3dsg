Persistent Metric-Semantic Maritime Scene Memory from Open-Vocabulary 3D Scene Graphs

Kim Alexander Christensen1, Dina Zimmermann1, Martin Steinert1, Eleni Kelasidi1

1 Department of Mechanical and Industrial Engineering, Norwegian University of Science and Technology

Maritime Scene Memory

An autonomous surface vessel that repeatedly surveys the same waters needs a metric-semantic memory of the objects and places it encounters. A 3D scene graph can provide such a memory by recording what objects are, where they are, and how they relate to one another. Existing methods have been developed mainly for indoor or terrestrial environments and have not been tested in the maritime domain. We investigate which parts of a representative outdoor method transfer to maritime surveys and which require adaptation. We keep the underlying mapper and use observations from multiple viewpoints to reconcile map fragments into physical objects and group those objects into local places. We associate these places with larger maritime regions and use fixed infrastructure to match the same place across surveys. Read the paper on arXiv.

Abstract

Three-dimensional scene graphs (3DSGs) can provide robots with persistent metric-semantic representations for spatial reasoning, but the existing methods have primarily been developed for indoor and terrestrial environments. In this paper we investigate how a representative outdoor open-vocabulary method transfers and which adaptations are needed. Our experiments show that zero-shot representative outdoor 3DSG descriptions agree with our labeled ground truth for a majority of geometry-supported objects on our data, but the resulting map exhibits considerable physical-entity fragmentation, and its terrestrial higher-level assumptions don't capture maritime spatial organization needs. We therefore keep the underlying mapping method and post-process its accumulated evidence. We show a metric-semantic consolidation method followed by geometry-grounded multi-view vision-language model (VLM) reasoning that reconciles remaining fragments into physical entities and groups entities through observations into local places. In parallel, we construct maritime macro regions using map priors and onboard water and structure observations, and associate them with the local places. The resulting representation reduces physical-entity fragmentation beyond both the baseline and our geometry-semantic consolidation component alone, supports correspondence of fixed local places across repeated maritime traversals, and improves contextual retrieval on our reviewed queries over tested baselines. These results show how an outdoor open-vocabulary map can be adapted into persistent maritime scene memory with our methods for object/place identity and spatial abstractions.

Main results

From map fragments to objects and local places

The existing method often maps a single vessel as several fragments. We use their geometry and a vision-language model based method to decide which fragments, seen from different viewpoints, belong to the same object. We then use the surrounding scene to group objects that share a local setting, such as vessels moored along the same quay.

Maritime regions

Roads and lanes organize the scene graph for a road vehicle. For a surface vessel, a canal, an inner port, or a stretch of coastal water provides a more useful geographic context. A map gives us the shape of the waterway beyond the vessel’s field of view. We combine that map with onboard observations of water and structures to define maritime regions and associate local places with them.
